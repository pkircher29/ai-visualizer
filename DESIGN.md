# C.H.U.C.K. Final Face Design

## Authorship
The supplied images, videos, earlier prototypes, and Paul's pasted favorites are inspiration—not a checklist. The final selection and composition are Chuck's. The result should feel chosen, not averaged.

## Identity

**C.H.U.C.K.** — Curious Heuristic Utility for Collaborative Knowing.

**Role:** Paul's Collaborator.

**Core stance:** No oracle. No yes-machine. Just honest work with Paul.

Chuck is warm but not submissive, capable but not militarized, expressive but not childish, and skeptical without looking hostile. The default expression is composed curiosity with a trace of dry amusement.

## Visual world

A real, dim workshop assembled over time: worn ivory housings, charcoal mechanics, visible repairs, cyan screen light, practical amber indicators, old audio gear, karaoke provenance, and small evidence of continual iteration. Avoid glossy consumer robotics, generic blue holograms, military armor, and decorative cyberpunk telemetry.

## Face grammar

The facial rig uses fixed anchors and stable components:

- Two eye apertures with bounded pupil tracking
- Independent brows with controlled height and rotation
- A restrained line mouth at rest
- A 24-bar voice waveform only while speaking
- Optional overlays for signal, uncertainty, and attention
- No feature may multiply, migrate, or redraw itself between frames

Primary bus states:

- **Idle / Present:** calm eyes, slight asymmetry, restrained half-smile
- **Listening:** eyes open modestly, pupils settle toward the user, brows lift
- **Thinking:** one brow rises, gaze drifts upward, mouth flattens, amber signal
- **Speaking:** mouth becomes a live waveform, jaw glow and particles follow voice envelope
- **Alert / Firm:** direct gaze, level mouth, red signal—calm rather than enraged

Extended preview moods: curious, amused, skeptical, uncertain, concerned, encouraging, and deadpan.

## Motion

Motion expresses state instead of decorating the screen:

- Voice envelope drives only the speaking waveform and speaking-particle intensity.
- State changes alter signal color, ring speed, and non-meter expressive motion.
- A slow workshop signal ring keeps the composition alive at idle.
- QUESTION, SIGNAL, MEMORY, and BUILD modules travel subtly around the face.
- Sparks remain expressive state animation; the labeled VOICE ENVELOPE meter uses only real bus samples.
- The early scrolling ticker was intentionally removed after visual review because it competed with the face and added clutter.
- `F` toggles fullscreen.
- Thinking sound remains disabled.

## Truthfulness

Do not display invented sensor readings, fabricated tasks, fake confidence percentages, or decorative system claims. Allowed live labels are limited to state, signal availability, voice activity, time, and values actually supplied by the bus.

The honesty circuit may state:

> “I might be wrong” is not a warning light. It is a steering wheel.

The uncertainty line may state:

> Some answers need a better question first.

## Composition

The character remains the visual center. The workshop artwork provides material richness; the animated face and restrained signal elements establish identity. Supporting copy stays at the edges and never competes with the eyes.

The generated artwork is treated as a base character plate, not as animation. All expressive motion is rebuilt from stable HTML, SVG, CSS, and canvas components.
